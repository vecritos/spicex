## Conversion Process

Focusing on integration and conversion rather than building new we can update our cities.
Finding and replacing old systems then becomes a problem of recollection and recycling.
This replaces the old problems of destroying, repairing, and maintaining buildings with the same problems for small units.

The existing infrastructure is made to allow for interchangeability of shelter components, and each sits within a larger framework.
The framework is made to be highly durable, and quick connections and lock-in mechanisms allow for the exchanges.
Architecture and design are still included and the framework shells become how to make the frame interesting.
Remember the frame is one of our essential ingredients to shelters.

Rather than rebuild an entire building, we simply swap out pieces designed to fit in place.
A current day example of this is Urban Science and ChannelVantage moving floors or redesigning in the Renaissance Center, Detroit, Michigan.
A futuristic example is individual compartments that are simply lifted and inserted into each floor.

## Finding a Niche

If we design the minimal shelter and build them robust, durable, and simple we can place them anywhere, even the moon.
Over time this allows us to create more semi-private and public spaces that can be shared for all.
Think about having a favorite quiet book nook tucked deep into a library.

Here are a few examples of where shelters can be made.

- Densely packed into cities
- Excavated lakes with small air bubble pods underwater.
- Close to coral reefs, the drop off is prime real-estate.
- Up in age old trees or between many trees 
- On platform vessels connected by rope bridges

You'll notice I have a lot of water examples, this is because many humans have done a terrible job maintaining the planet.
Movements into water locations and reallocation of natural lands to nature parks would allow the land to recover.
Designing a planet for environmentalism and working with our fellow carbon species will be beneficial long-term.
