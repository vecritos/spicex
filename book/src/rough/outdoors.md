# Planning and Packing

## Outdoor Essentials

Bring essential ingredients to a successful trip but make sure to remember the downside if you don't use them or fail to plan and prepare.
We will go over a few essential pieces of equipment as well as common practices on the trail.
Let this be an introduction into the basics, there is unlimited room for expansion in the backcountry.

## Map and Compass -- Plan and Prepare
  
In unknown locations it's always good to have a backup plan.
Contemporary society has taught us to use our phones at all time.
In constant use, we have lost our ability to have a solid sense of direction.
Even around home, it is good to get the lay of the land.
If we stay digital, using our devices to study the map can lead to discovery of new places.
New places could even be a coffee shop you did not know existed or a hidden ATM that comes in handy when needed.

In the backcountry paper maps are essential, we cannot afford to rely on battery devices.
And this is why keeping an Atlas in your car is a great idea, just in case you get stuck on the road.

It is always good to plan and prepare.
Have a base idea of what trip you want to go on.
It can be a backcountry trip, an urban outing, or a local park.
Go over what you are bringing and make sure it will be everything you need.
In time you find yourself happier, because whatever you come across, you have ready.

## Sun and Insect Protection

Sun and insect protection can include sun resistant clothing and long sleeves.
It is good to bring some bug spray, although offloading the liquid into small dropper bottles can be helpful too.
Choose what is right for the weather.
Even in the cities, there are still bugs, but not always.
If it is the winter you can nix, or remove, the insect protection as they will not be out.
Also make sure to bring sunglasses in the winter, you can actually burn your retinas with sunlight bouncing off the snow.

## Matches and Fire Starters

To burn a fire needs oxygen, fuel, and an ignition source.
In the woods, you can easily obtain a collection of **fallen** sticks and logs.
This wood will serve as your fuel in the campfire, hopefully oxygen is available.
At higher altitudes the ecosystem is more fragile so for open campfires keep this in mind.
Make sure to follow campfire procedures and minimize campfire impacts.
Some of these procedures include the following.

- Keeping the fire only as big as you need.
- Using pre-established campfire pits or creating a group one if necessary.
- Paying attention to fire-warnings, sometimes these are issued to keep the risk of forest fires down.
- Making sure the fire is completely out before leaving.

## First Aid Kit

Always have a first aid kit.
Even in urban settings having a small band-aid can be a great feeling in case of a paper cut.
We will go over more about what to keep in your first aid kit and what to do in first-aid scenarios later on.
Keep one at home with replacement pieces is good as well.
Have a location everyone knows of.

## Rain Gear and Extra Layered Clothing
  
Bring extra clothes, a sudden cold snap could make your afternoon at the orchard miserable unless you have another layer.

When preparing for rain note that using muddy trails can often lead to erosion.
Check local signs before beginning a hike or excursion on a muddy trail.

## Water Bottles Filled with Disinfected Water

Stay hydrated.
Bring full ones everywhere but the airport.

## Tell Someone Your Itinerary

Sometimes you need a break, but let people know where you're going.
This becomes more important the longer you think you'll be gone.

## Extra Food, seriously pack extra food.

I've gone out too light a few times, it sucks, pack extra.

Bring some snacks.

## Pocket Knife

I use my knife all the time, even for opening envelopes, i barely notice it, but if you're packing light, razor blades work well.

## Headlamp or Flashlight
  
The night can be a great time to get things done.
It is a good idea to have backup lights in case you come across a dark alley, or the lightbulb burns out.
Have a backup light for urban settings.

The red light mode won't damage your night vision as much.
The blacklight helps for accident awareness like spotting blood.
