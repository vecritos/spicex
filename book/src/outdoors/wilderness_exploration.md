# Advice

- camp away from water and cook area hidden from view
- clean up trash, pack it out, use the sump, leave what you find
- respect wildlife, never provoke a bear
- store fuel, smellables, food in hung bear bag at night
- build structures well and for great reasons
- be kind

# Planning and Packing

Bring essential ingredients to a successful trip but make sure to remember the downside if you don't use them or fail to plan and prepare.

1. map and compass, plan and prepare
  - it's still good to bring a paper map in unknown locations, lets you learn the lay of the land, studying maps beforehand online can help to if traveling to a city
2. sun and insect protection
  - these can include garments needed for sun protection like a good hat to protect you from the sun
3. matches, minimize campfire impact
  - matches or some ignition source is good, think about candles as well even if not in the backcountry
4. first aid kit, respect wildlife
  - always have a first aid kit, keeping one at home with replacement pieces is good as well, have a location everyone knows of, same with the fire extinguisher 
5. rain gear & extra layered clothing, travel and camp on durable surfaces
  - muddy trails or rain can often lead to erosion, check local signs before beginning a hike on a muddy trail
6. water bottles filled with disinfected water
  - bring full ones everywhere but the airport
7. tell someone your itinerary, dispose of waste properly
  - sometimes you need a break, but let people know where you're going
8. extra food, seriously pack extra food.
  - i've gone out too light a few times, it sucks, pack extra
  > breakdown the calorie ranges consumed per day for different exertion levels
9. pocket knife, leave what you find
  - i use my knife all the time, even for opening envelopes, i barely notice it, but if you're packing light, razor blades work well
10. headlamp, be considerate of other visitors
