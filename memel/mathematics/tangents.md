# Tangents and Slope of Tangents

We wish to find the slope of a tangent line for point $P(a, f(a))$ but we only know one point on the line. Since the slope between $P$ and a point nearby on $f(x)$ approximates the tangent. We take the **limit** of this slope as the distance between the points approaches 0.

So the slope at $x=a$ is $\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ this is $\forall a$ used in the domain for $f(x)$ 

## Derivatives

$f'(x)$ is the derivative the slope at $x$ for $f(x)$ if $f'(x)$ exists $f$ is differentiable at $x=a$ this includes that the left hand derivative as $h \to 0^-$ is equal to $h \to 0^+$

### The Chain Rule

the derivative of $f(g(x))$ is $f'(g(x))g'(x)$


### Parametric Equations

$\frac{\delta y}{\delta x} = \frac{\delta y / \delta t}{\delta x / \delta t}$

### Implicit Differentiation

For situations where you cannot solve directly for $y$ you can use chain rule to add $y'$ or $\frac{\delta y}{\delta x}$ to the equation in place of the piece you do not know and algebraically reason it out from there
